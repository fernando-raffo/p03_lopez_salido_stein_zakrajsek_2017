\begin{abstract}
We replicate the core results of \citet{lopezsalido2017credit}, who show that elevated
credit-market sentiment forecasts a slowdown in real activity about two years ahead. We
describe the data, present our own summary statistics, and reproduce every table and
figure across the replication sample, an extended sample, and an Aaa-spread variant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview}
When credit is ``frothy''---the \citet{lopezsalido2017credit} thesis---spreads are narrow
and low-quality issuance is high; those conditions mean-revert as activity turns down.

\section{Data Sources}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FRED} \citep{fred}: Baa/Aaa yields, Treasury yields, GDP. Several series are a
newer vintage and must be stitched together; where the appendix is silent we made documented
judgement calls.
\item \textbf{Shiller} \citep{shiller2015irrational}: long-run market and P/E10 data.
\item \textbf{Greenwood--Hanson high-yield share} \citep{greenwood2013issuer}, pulled from the
HBS workbook \citep{hbsics} and spliced with Mergent FISD; a newer vintage than the paper.
\end{itemize}

\section{Successes and Challenges}
The long-run spread reproduces cleanly and the credit-beats-equity result holds
($R^2\approx0.38$). The main difficulties were sourcing the high-yield share and matching
the paper's \citet{newey1987simple} standard errors; vintage and series-stitching choices
explain small differences from the published numbers.
