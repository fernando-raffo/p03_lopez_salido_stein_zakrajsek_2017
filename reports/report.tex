\begin{abstract}
We replicate the core results of \citet{lopezsalido2017credit}, who show that elevated
credit-market sentiment forecasts a slowdown in real activity about two years ahead. We
describe the data, present summary statistics of the underlying series, and reproduce every
table and figure across the replication sample, an extended sample, and an Aaa-spread variant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview}

When credit is ``frothy''---the \citet{lopezsalido2017credit} thesis---spreads are narrow and
low-quality issuance is high; those conditions mean-revert as activity turns down.

\section{Data Sources}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FRED} \citep{fred}: Baa/Aaa yields, Treasury yields, GDP. Several series are a
newer vintage and must be stitched together across multiple FRED series; where the appendix is
silent about which underlying series to use, we made documented judgement calls.
\item \textbf{Shiller} \citep{shiller2015irrational}: long-run market data and the P/E10 ratio.
\item \textbf{Greenwood--Hanson high-yield share} \citep{greenwood2013issuer}, pulled from the
HBS workbook \citep{hbsics} and spliced with Mergent FISD \citep{mergentfisd}, accessed via
WRDS; a newer vintage than the paper.
\end{itemize}

\section{Case Study: The Sentiment Signal Out of Sample, 2020--2022}

\citep{mergentfisd} makes it possible to apply the Table II first stage, estimated on
1929--2015 and held fixed, to the COVID cycle. The figure below compares the predicted
change in the Baa--Treasury spread---using the full two-ingredient sentiment signal and,
separately, the spread-reversion leg alone---against the realized change. The full
walkthrough and its caveats are in \texttt{04\_case\_study.ipynb}.

\section{Successes and Challenges}

The long-run spread reproduces cleanly and the credit-beats-equity result holds
($R^2\approx0.38$). The main difficulties were sourcing the high-yield share and matching the
paper's \citet{newey1987simple} standard errors; vintage and series-stitching choices explain
small differences from the published numbers.
